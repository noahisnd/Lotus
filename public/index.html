The details we think you’ll care about

A schedule can start a session, but never end one.

Sleep mode can switch on at 22:00 on weeknights without you deciding to switch it on. There is no matching end time, because that would be a second way out that does not need the key.

You cannot weaken it mid-session.

Once a session is running, the settings are locked too. No quietly removing a site from the list to get around your own block.

Blocking happens in three places at once.

Sites are blocked at two different levels of your system, and blocked apps are closed if you open them. Any one of those on its own would leak.

The usual workarounds are already closed.

The tricks people reach for first, private DNS, hidden browser settings, proxy and VPN services, are shut off while a session runs, along with about 17,500 sites that exist purely to get around blockers.

Browser settings stay changed after a restart.

Browsers only read their settings when they open, so a change that lasted only as long as the session would be useless five seconds after you quit Chrome.

The app cannot let itself out.

The window you click on has no power to unlock anything. It can only ask, and be refused. There is no prompt you can talk yourself into approving.

Questions

What is Stick?

Software for your Mac or Windows PC that blocks the apps and websites you find distracting, and a USB key that ends the block. While a session is running, the only way to unlock your computer and get those apps and websites back is to plug in the key.

What happens if I lose the key?

Five emergency unlocks per machine. It’s a lifetime allowance and they don’t refill. After that, write to us and we’ll unlock it manually.

How is this different from Screen Time or Freedom?

Their key lives on the machine you’re trying to get away from. Ours is an object you can put in another room. Some of those apps are legitimately hard to cancel mid-session, and we’re not claiming otherwise. We’re claiming distance beats difficulty.

Can’t I just get around it?

Yes, if you’re determined and technical. It’s your computer and you have administrator rights on it. Stick is built to beat the impulse, not you with a terminal open and an afternoon free. What it removes is the easy exit: no timer to wait out, no button that ends a session early, and no password you can talk yourself into typing. The way back in is a walk to wherever you left the key.

Do I need the key to start a session?

No. Start one from the menu bar, or let a schedule do it. The key is only how you get out.

What can I block?

Any website and any app, individually. Plus two bundled category lists, Explicit content and Gambling, which are tens of thousands of domains each. There deliberately isn’t a “social media” category: the site picker lets you choose the individual sites, which is finer-grained and more honest than one switch over a list you can’t inspect.

Does the block survive a restart?

Yes. The blocking service runs at boot and is set to restart if it stops, the blocked-hosts region is restored within two seconds of any hand edit, and browser policy is written persistently rather than per-session. Restarting, logging out, quitting the app or killing the process all leave the block in place. What ends it is the key, or one of the five emergency unlocks.

What does Stick install?

A small program that runs in the background, plus the app window you use. The background program needs the highest level of access on your machine, because that is what keeps a block in place after a restart instead of letting it disappear the moment you quit the app. It is signed either way — checked by Apple on a Mac, and code-signed on Windows — so your computer can confirm it came from us and has not been tampered with, and the installer comes on the key itself. The window you click on has no power to unlock anything by itself: it can only ask the background program, and be refused.

Do you see what I block?

No. Nothing in Stick sends anything to us: no account, no check to see whether you paid, no record of how you use it. Your modes, your blocked lists and your session history stay on your machine. It does download two lists from GitHub, and neither carries anything about you: the sites for a category when you switch one on, and a weekly refresh of the list of sites people use to get around blockers. If you would rather check than take our word for it, any network monitor will show you exactly that.
